## 1. Park Basics

- **Name / designation:** Acadia National Park (`acad`), Maine
- **What it protects:** the highest rocky headlands along the US Atlantic coastline, abundant habitats, and a rich cultural heritage
- **Trail/road inventory:** 158 miles of hiking trails, 27 miles of historic motor roads, 45 miles of carriage roads
- **Visitation:** ~4 million visits/year — one of the top 10 most-visited US national parks
- **Open:** year-round, 24/7 (facilities are seasonal — see §4)
- **Main visitor contact station:** Hulls Cove Visitor Center, 25 Visitor Center Road, Bar Harbor, ME 04609
- **Contacts:** (207) 288-3338 (voice) · (207) 288-8813 (fax) · (207) 288-8800 (TTY) · acadia_information@nps.gov

### Directions (from Boston)
I-95 north to Augusta, ME, then Route 3 east to Ellsworth and on to Mount Desert Island. Alternate: continue I-95 to Bangor, take I-395 to US-1A east to Ellsworth, then Route 3 to Mount Desert Island.

### Entrance fees & passes
| Item | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Private vehicle | $35.00 | Valid 7 days; ≤15-passenger vehicles incl. rentals/RVs/vans |
| Motorcycle | $30.00 | Valid 7 days; up to 2 motorcycles / 4 passengers |
| Per person (foot/bike) | $20.00 | Valid 7 days; youth 15 and under free |
| Timed-entry vehicle reservation | $6.00 | Location-specific (Cadillac area); per vehicle; road closes to vehicles 9 pm; no re-entry; no assigned parking; not required for pedestrians/bikes/taxis; Cadillac is NOT served by Island Explorer |
| Non-US residents 16+ | $100.00 | In addition to standard entrance fee; waived with Annual or America the Beautiful pass |
| Acadia Annual Pass | $70.00 | Acadia-only, 12 months; available online, at park visitor centers/campgrounds, and at area chambers of commerce |

Group/bus notes: education groups may qualify for a fee waiver; non-commercial groups (16+) pay $20/person; commercial operators need a Commercial Use Authorization.

### Weather (official)
- **Fall: 30–70 °F (−1 to 21 °C)** — first frost typically mid-October
- First snowfall typically begins in November and continues through April (avg. accumulation 73 in / 185 cm); winter 14–35 °F
- Summer 45–90 °F; spring 30–70 °F
- **Trip takeaway:** pack cold-weather layers, rain gear, and traction-considerate footwear; expect short daylight hours

### Representative activities (full NPS list)
Hiking (front-country), camping, group camping, biking, boating, boat/bus tours, climbing, freshwater/saltwater/fly fishing, paddling (canoe/kayak/SUP), picnicking, swimming, wildlife watching, birdwatching, stargazing/astronomy, guided tours, geocaching, horseback riding, Junior Ranger, park film, and more.

### 2.1 Eastern Section of Beech Mountain Loop Trail — closed for repairs
Closed from a junction just beyond the parking lot to just below the summit at the fire tower. **Three alternate routes to the summit remain open:** the outer loop of the Beech Mountain Loop Trail, Beech South Ridge Trail, and Beech West Ridge Trail.

### 2.2 Schoodic Mountain Road — closed for repairs
Closed from Schoodic Loop Road to the summit of Schoodic Head. Workers operating heavy equipment for resurfacing, regrading, and ditching. (Schoodic Peninsula is also home to Schoodic Woods Campground.)

### 2.3 Southern Section of Ocean Path — closed for repairs
Closed from the trailhead across from the Otter Point parking lot to the trail's southern terminus. (Oldest alert update — may resolve soon; verify before departure.)

### Planning impact
- No closures affect the Park Loop Road itself, the carriage roads, or Cadillac Mountain.
- If Beech Mountain is on the itinerary: ascend via South Ridge or West Ridge.
- If doing the Schoodic Peninsula: plan to hike up Schoodic Head from Schoodic Loop Road rather than drive.
- The classic Ocean Path shoreline stretch is interrupted at its southern (Otter Point) end — consider starting at Sand Beach, or substitute Gorham Mountain-area trails.
- All three closures are repair zones with heavy equipment — keep out of work areas.

## 3. Campgrounds (4 total) — all seasonal; key dates for a fall trip

**Common reservation rules (Blackwoods, Seawall, Schoodic Woods):**
- Reservation-only via Recreation.gov — **no walk-up/first-come-first-served sites; no phone or in-person booking**
- 90% of sites released 6 months in advance, on the 1st of each month at 10 am EST
- Remaining 10% released at 10 am EST, 14 days ahead of arrival date, rolling
- An entrance pass is required **in addition to** campground fees

### 3.1 Blackwoods Campground — 155 Blackwoods Dr, Otter Creek
- East side of Mount Desert Island, ~5 mi south of Bar Harbor on Route 3; wooded sites within a 10-minute walk of the ocean
- **Season: early May to mid-October** · 281 sites (221 tent-only, 60 RV-only, 4 group; no electrical hookups)
- Fees: tent/RV non-electric $30/night; group tent area $60/night
- Amenities: flush toilets (seasonal), potable water, dump station, camp store, ice & firewood for sale, food storage lockers, amphitheater, cell/internet reception, laundry; **no showers**
- Accessibility: 12 accessible drive-in sites, RV max 35 ft, paved roads, hard-packed walkways to accessible restrooms/amphitheater
- Phone: (207) 288-3274 · https://www.nps.gov/acad/planyourvisit/blackwoods-campground.htm · https://www.recreation.gov/camping/campgrounds/232508

### 3.2 Seawall Campground — 664 Seawall Rd, Southwest Harbor
- West side of Mount Desert Island, ~4 mi south of Southwest Harbor, ~18 mi from Bar Harbor; sites within short walking distance of the coast
- **Season: Wednesday before Memorial Day through Columbus Day (Oct 12, 2026)** · ranger station generally 9 am–8 pm peak season (staffing-dependent)
- Fees: standard/RV non-electric $30/night; group tent area $60/night
- https://www.nps.gov/acad/planyourvisit/seawall-campground.htm · https://www.recreation.gov/camping/campgrounds/234674

### 3.3 Schoodic Woods Campground — 54 Farview Dr, Winter Harbor
- Newest campground; Schoodic Peninsula, 1.5 mi SE of Winter Harbor; **60–70 min drive from Bar Harbor**
- **Season: Wednesday before Memorial Day weekend through Columbus Day** · 89 sites (78 with electrical hookups: 41 RV-only, 13 tent-only, 2 group, 9 walk/boat-to); 78 ADA-accessible sites
- Fees: hike-in $22 · standard electric $30 · tent-only electric $30 · RV electric no water $36 · RV electric+water $40 · group tent $60
- Same amenities as Blackwoods (no showers) · Phone: (207) 288-1300
- https://www.nps.gov/acad/planyourvisit/camping.htm · https://www.recreation.gov/camping/campgrounds/251833

### 3.4 Duck Harbor Campground — Isle au Haut (backcountry)
- **5 lean-to shelter sites**; remote island campground reached by mailboat from Stonington; no vehicle access
- Reservations required — entire season released 10 am on April 1 (7 months ahead) · $20/night, max 6 people/site
- Rules: max 3 nights, one stay per calendar year, no pets, no generators, pack out all trash, no outside firewood; check-in/out 11 am; if arriving late, call (207) 335-5551 to avoid a no-show fee; quiet hours 10 pm–8 am
- https://www.nps.gov/acad/planyourvisit/duckharbor.htm · https://www.recreation.gov/camping/campgrounds/264293

### Fall-trip takeaway
If trip dates are after Columbus Day (Oct 12, 2026), the vehicle-accessible campgrounds will likely be closed — pivot to lodging in Bar Harbor/Southwest Harbor, or camp before the closures. Watch Recreation.gov for the last-10% releases if dates are before mid-October.

| Facility | Location | Hours | Season end (2026) |

| Islesford Historical Museum | Little Cranberry Island (boat only) | 10:30 am–4 pm daily | Sep 19, 2026 (closed) |

| Winter info: Bar Harbor Chamber of Commerce | 2 Cottage St, Bar Harbor | 8 am–4 pm daily | Opens ~Nov 1 (into early May) |

**Details:**
- **Hulls Cove VC:** Acadia's main visitor contact station and transportation hub (May–Oct); adjacent to a Park Loop Road entrance; key Island Explorer stop; 52 steps to the main entrance with a separate accessible entrance; (207) 288-3338.
- **Sieur de Monts Nature Center:** visitor services, hiking info, interactive exhibits; trailhead for many Sieur de Monts-area hikes; rangers present spring–fall.
- **Acadia Gateway Center:** intermodal transit/welcome center (Island Explorer stop, day-use parking, tourism info, store, restrooms); state-owned, DOT-operated in partnership with NPS, Downeast Transportation, and Maine Office of Tourism.
- **Islesford Historical Museum:** Cranberry Isles cultural history, 1700s–present; boat access only; left of the Town Pier in Islesford.
- **Schoodic Institute Welcome Center at Rockefeller Hall:** Schoodic Peninsula; exhibits on the old Navy base plus park information; 10 am–5 pm, open 7 days/week through Oct 26, 2026.
- **Winter information:** Bar Harbor Chamber of Commerce, 2 Cottage St (corner of Cottage & Main), 8 am–4 pm daily, typically Nov 1–early May; full-day closures on Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve/Day, New Year's Eve/Day.

**Fall-trip takeaway:** for a late-October trip, Hulls Cove VC is the last NPS-staffed facility standing (until end of October); after that, info moves to the Bar Harbor Chamber of Commerce. Plan arrival stops within the 8:30 am–4:30 pm window.

## 5. Events — next three weeks (Oct 19 – Nov 9, 2026) — 4 total

### 5.1 Drop-in Stewardship Volunteering with Friends of Acadia (Regular Event)
- **Dates:** Oct 20, 22, 24, 27, 29, 31 — Tue/Thu/Sat through end of October, 8:15 am–12:15 pm, weather permitting
- Meet/park: Hulls Cove Storage Facility, near Hulls Cove Visitor Center (Park HQ is under construction; same as 2024). Enter via the Hulls Cove VC entrance off Route 3, continue past the VC lot on Paradise Hill Rd, bear left at the split (past the "authorized vehicles only" signs — volunteer signs will guide you)
- Vegetation cutting, drainage clearing, bogwalk building, tread work, weeding carriage roads; led by volunteer crew leaders; **no experience or reservation required**

### 5.2 Wetland Revival – Walking the Path of Restoration (Regular Event, ranger-led)
- **Dates:** Oct 21, 23, 28, 30; meet outside Sieur de Monts Nature Center
- Mostly level 2.3-mile ranger-led walk through the Great Meadow on wetland restoration by Acadia, the Schoodic Institute, and Friends of Acadia
- Accessibility accommodations (sign language, live audio description, assistive listening) with 10 days' advance notice: 207-288-3338 option 0, or acadia_information@nps.gov

### 5.3 EeeK of Ecology Halloween Festival for Children (Special Event)
- **Date:** Oct 24, all day — Schoodic Institute campus, 64 Acadia Drive, Winter Harbor
- Trick-or-treat learning stations about national parks; spiders, endangered/invasive species, ghosts; Creepy Craft Corner. For children 12 and under with an adult.

### 5.4 Take Pride in Acadia Day (Special Event)
- **Date:** Nov 7, 2026 — Hulls Cove area (44.409137, -68.248873)
- Volunteers rake leaves out of carriage-road drainage ditches to prevent leaf dams and clogged culverts ("putting the carriage roads to bed for the season"); ends with chili, corn bread, cider, and cake. All ages.

**Takeaway:** club-relevant options are #1 (group volunteering) and #2 (easy ranger-led walk); #4 is a strong service-day candidate if the trip overlaps Nov 7. Ranger programming winds down after October.

## 6. What the official NPS park page adds (beyond the structured data)

- **Vehicle Reservations** — dedicated section under Permits & Reservations for timed-entry vehicle reservations (Cadillac area, $6/vehicle), consistent with the fee data above
- **Island Explorer** — dedicated pages for the fare-free bus system (note: Cadillac is not served by Island Explorer)
- **Oversized Vehicles** — guidance for RVs/large vehicles
- **Travel & Parking Forecast Tools** and the **Park App** — tools to check expected crowds/parking before heading out (valuable for a top-10 most-visited park)
- **Current Conditions** page — the authoritative place to re-verify alerts/closures before each day
- **Calendar** and **"Attend A Ranger Program"** sections — for event listings beyond the three-week window queried here
- **Stargazing** is a highlighted activity — Acadia has notable night-sky resources (a good evening activity for the club)
- **Visiting With Your Pet** and **Safety** sections — worth reviewing for group policy
- Extensive history/nature sections (Wabanaki Nations, Fire of 1947, carriage roads and gatehouses, lighthouses, etc.) if we want interpretive talking points for the group
